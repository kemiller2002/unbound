**Gaps in the method that affect this application (stated, not repaired).**
- Confidence Debt is "Core" but has **no definition anywhere in the repository**; Burden Externalization, Governance Debt and Resolution Debt have only one-line notes (clarity-framework@200a9eb:research/2026-framework-improvement/baseline/current-limitations.md:131). Where this evaluation uses them, the working reading is stated inline and is the evaluator's interpretation: *Confidence Debt* = confidence carried forward above what evidence supports; *Assumption Drift* = "an assumption gradually becomes treated as fact" (clarity-framework@200a9eb:docs/framework-evolution/apollo-challenger-reassessment.md:177-179); *Trigger Drift* = "a signal gradually stops producing reassessment" (same file, 171); *Burden Externalization* = work or risk moved onto parties outside the decision; *Governance Debt* = controls or reviews that are required but not resourced.
- **Clarity's own evidence boundary.** On a held-out 50-case benchmark, current Clarity was "no more accurate than the same model with no method", "much less useful" (2.2 vs 3.9 of 4) and longer; "its one clear strength is making assumptions explicit" (clarity-framework@200a9eb:research/2026-framework-improvement/README.md:19-21). Its gains were reproduced by a plain three-sentence instruction (clarity-framework@200a9eb:research/2026-framework-improvement/README.md:37). **Consequence for this report:** the Clarity results below are reasoning maps whose main value is assumption exposure; they are not evidence that any recommendation is right.
- **Disagreement between the two frameworks about Clarity's role.** EDF's README says "Clarity answers: What should we do?" (echelon-diagnostic-framework@710d54c:docs/edf/README.md:13-15) while Clarity says it is not primarily a decision framework. Preserved, not resolved. Here Clarity is used for calibration of commitment, not as an approval gate -- consistent with the reconciliation's ruling "Use their vocabulary ... in findings, not as gates" (unbound:requirements/reconciliation/2026-11-23/13-ARCHITECTURE-DEPENDENCY-ANALYSIS.md:48).

**Cross-cutting systemic observations.**
1. **Circular evidence / Confidence Debt chain.** One model family researched, reviewed, decided (R7), scoped (R5) and now evaluates. R6 packs embed R7 positions; R5 depends on R6 and R7; R1 and R8 economics depend on R4 Tier-1 legality (R7-adjacent counsel Q1). A single overturned position can propagate through four recommendations. Control: machine-enforced provisional basis (UNB-SMP-CLR-002).
2. **Goodhart risk.** STP rate and cost per account are targets and measures at once; with counter-metric floors deferred out of R1 (report 17:19), nothing halts a cost gain that comes from weaker detection. Finding SPF-CLR-010.
3. **Burden externalization.** Minimum-touch, self-service, charity self-maintenance and outsourced specialists all move work; the mission principle says moved work is not eliminated. Only one R1 metric (repeat contacts via counter-metrics) would see it, and the floors are deferred.
4. **Common-mode failure.** Shared registry + shared packs + one AI vendor = correlated failure across sponsors, which a minimum-touch organization is least staffed to absorb. Architecture section addresses placement and staging.
5. **Fallback capacity paradox.** Every automated path's safe fallback is "hold" or "human review". At December volume human review is not a feasible fallback for a small team ([D] ~1,333 reviewer-hours per day at 10 min/grant for 8,000 grants), so the true fallback is hold-and-notify, whose cost is donor/charity friction. This must be designed, not discovered (UNB-SMP-ARX-001).

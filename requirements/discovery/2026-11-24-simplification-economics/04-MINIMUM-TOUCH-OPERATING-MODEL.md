## Minimum-touch operating model

*Source pass: Work inventory, operating model and staffing.*

Four models per function. *Conventional* = departmental, human review of most items. *Improved conventional* = same organization with better tools. *AI-native* = the baseline design (deterministic routine + AI flags + human exceptions). *Minimum-touch* = the smallest human footprint that keeps every legal, fiduciary and independent-control duty, with elimination and prevention applied before automation. **Cross-cutting rules for the minimum-touch model.**
1. A human touch exists only when it falls in one of the human-intervention classes (next section) and the creating rule says so (UNB-SMP-ORG-001).
2. Deterministic before AI; AI output is evidence, never authority (REQ-X-017; RR-AIN-003-r1).
3. Automation failure fails safe to hold or to an eligible human — never fail-open (REQ-X-017 acceptance).
4. Every exception class has a prevention owner; recurring exceptions become REQ-X-042 proposals.
5. The minimum-touch model adds an independent sampling control (UNB-XIN-CLM-005) because fewer humans see routine decisions; its cost is counted.

Eliminated ≈ **38,950 h/yr ≈ 24.3 FTE** at H = 1,600 h [A] (economic-model.md:32), a 84% reduction. The residual is dominated by **ED and ER** (SPW-053/054): legally human, reducible only by routing (US intermediaries, reusable ED advice within its ~2-year currency) — not by automation.

**Cross-check against sourced cost pools [S] (industry/grt.md §8, which cites daf-landscape sponsor-cost-structure files):**
- Routine eligibility check for a listed domestic public charity ≈ **$0.14 per grant** (unbound:requirements/discovery/2026-11-23-cross-industry/industry/grt.md:206). At 800k grants [S baseline] that is ≈ **$112k/yr** [D] — consistent with the inventory's view that the routine lookup at a large national is already near-automated. **The conventional column above (≈6,667 h for per-grant lookup) represents a staff-checked process (community-foundation-like), where anchored labour is $180–323 per grant versus $7.9 at large nationals (grt.md:222).** The saving claimed by minimum-touch vetting is therefore large relative to conventional/community-foundation practice and small relative to large nationals.
- Non-standard diligence (ER, disqualified SOs, international ED) ≈ **$1.25M per 1M grants** (grt.md:207) → ≈ **$1.0M/yr** at 800k grants [D]. This is the pool that minimum-touch cannot automate; the grt.md implication (lines 224-229) is that savings come from routing exceptions to cheaper lawful paths, eliminating checks, dropping agreements and receipts on routine grants — **"automating routine verification further adds little."** This analysis agrees.
- Human approval ≈ $13 and manual exception ≈ $46 illustrative unit costs at $1/min (industry/svc.md:173-174) — consistent with 13 and 46 minutes per touch.
- Multi-sponsor reuse: with *s* sponsors sharing a recipient population with overlap *o*, the factual-verification portion scales as ≈ 1/(1 + o(s−1)) per sponsor [D formula], but the per-sponsor ED/ER, eligibility and reliance work does not shrink. Because the factual portion is already the cheap part, cross-sponsor reuse matters mainly for **ED reuse** — and that is the legally unverified part (SPF-ORG-008).

**Conclusion.** The 180-day automated design is sound as a *sponsor policy* with continuous event monitoring and dual-point checks; the big remaining cost is legal non-standard diligence, which is a routing and policy problem, not an automation problem.

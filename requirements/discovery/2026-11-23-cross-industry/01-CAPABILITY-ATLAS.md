# 01: Cross-industry capability atlas (deliverable 1)

This atlas maps capabilities found in overlapping industries. For each capability, the full record in `data/capability-atlas.jsonl` answers the twelve mission §4 questions:

1. What business problem does it solve?
2. Which industries require it, and why?
3. Does a DAF have an equivalent problem?
4. Is it universal, or specific to certain sponsor models?
5. Is it necessary for legal, fiduciary, contractual, operational, commercial or merely conventional reasons?
6. Which existing requirements cover it?
7. Is the coverage concrete enough?
8. Is our workflow more complicated than it needs to be?
9. Can it be automated, and how?
10. What risks does automation introduce?
11. What evidence would validate it?
12. Where does the analogy break down?

The table below abbreviates each record. Read the JSONL for full text and evidence.

**Method.** Each industry was assessed independently by one research pass before any comparison was made. Those passes are in [`industry/`](industry/). The comparison step then rejected analogies that rested only on shared terminology. Every row records where its analogy breaks down. Several analogies were rejected outright:

- **Funds transfer pricing** has no deposit book to price ([industry/eco.md](industry/eco.md)).
- **Customer due diligence (CDD)** runs the wrong way for a DAF. The sponsor is the bank's customer, not a bank ([industry/bnk.md](industry/bnk.md)).
- **Claimant entitlement** does not exist for donors, because the sponsor holds exclusive legal control ([industry/svc.md](industry/svc.md)).
- **ACATS** moves a customer's own account between brokers. A gift changes ownership, so it is not an ACATS transfer.
- **Transfer-agency investor servicing** does not apply because there are no external unitholders.

# 16 — Synthetic operating year and internal time study

**Decision.** OD-M-14 (2026-11-25): no design-partner sponsor will be available. The first paying sponsor's contract
carries the report-12 evidence obligations. Until then, handling-time evidence comes from Unbound's own team working a
synthetic operating year. Segment commitments are a board decision on reports 14 and 16 (CNV-ECO-003 amended through
`clauses/c15`).

**What this measures and what it cannot.** The internal study measures how long Unbound's own people take to clear each
kind of work item on Unbound's workflow, and how accurately they do it. It cannot measure a sponsor's staff, a sponsor's
existing systems or real donor behaviour. Those come from the first paying sponsor.

## 1. The synthetic operating year

Generated by `replay/` with `synth` (F#): deterministic from a seed, with no live data. The generated tables are in
[report 16a](16a-SYNTHETIC-OPERATING-YEAR-TABLES.md); the summary and input hashes are in
`replay/out/synthetic/summary.json`.

| Element | Source | Evidence label |
|---|---|---|
| Funds: 10,000, a mid-size national sponsor (CNV-ECO-004 entry segment) | Configuration | Policy |
| Donor segments and their grant, contribution, contact, complex-share and check rates | Economics model `parameters.json` (segments, `market` mix) | As labelled in the model (mostly A) |
| December surge: arrivals ×3 over the last 25 days | `december_k`, `december_days` | A (TVA-004) |
| Reconciliation breaks, ACH returns, new-fund share | `recon_break_rate`, `ach_return_rate`, `new_account_share` | A |
| Complex assets: a quarter of the segment share, 1 hour of intake each | OD-M-09 policy (as in `EnterpriseEconomics.fs`) | Policy |
| Grant recipients: the 105,013 real recipients from the replay, weighted by how often sponsors grant to them | Public Form 990 data (report 14) | Market data |
| Donor loyalty: 60% of a fund's grants go to its own 3–8 recurring recipients | Configuration | Illustrative |
| Amounts: log-normal around the segment averages (σ 1.0 grants, 1.2 contributions) | Configuration | Illustrative |
| Screening: every grant routed by the report-14 policy and token-weighted sanctions matcher; repeat exception recipients become "reuse" items | `Screening.fs`, CNV-GRT-006 | Design |
| Injected known-bad grants: revoked, private foundation and sanctioned names, 0.5 per 1,000 grants each | Configuration | Test design |

All model inputs are read from `parameters.json`, so changing a model parameter changes the synthetic year too. The
values labelled illustrative are generator settings, not claims about donors.

## 2. Results

These figures come from 16a; the generated tables there are authoritative.

| Measure | Value | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Grants / contributions | 71,475 / 64,932 | 7.1 grants per fund, inside the model's sourced range (6.2–13 per account) |
| Grants routed to a person | 10.9% (6.3% first-time, 4.6% reused within the year) | Consistent with the public-data replay (11.2%) |
| December | 19.5% of grants; peak week 2.5× the mean | Sizes surge staffing (TEV-001) |
| Modelled hours | About 5,350 a year, about 3.3 FTE at 1,600 productive hours | Model minutes per item; **the internal study replaces these** |

Within a single year, reuse covers 42% of exceptions. The replay measured 64% when the prior year's history is also
available. The synthetic year starts with no history, so it shows a sponsor's first year on Unbound.

**Injected known-bad grants (16a §3).** All 30 revoked recipients and all 29 private foundations reached a person. Of
the 38 sanctioned names, 36 reached a person and **2 passed straight through**. A missed name never reaches a
reviewer, so the internal study cannot detect these misses; only the matcher acceptance suite can (OD-M-10). This is the
report-14 recall gap, seen end to end.

## 3. Internal time study protocol

**Sample.** `replay/out/synthetic/time-study-sample.jsonl`, deterministic and stratified:

- up to 40 items of each kind (exception first-time and reuse, block review, sanctions alert, complex intake, contact, reconciliation break, ACH return, onboarding review);
- 30 injected known-bad cases, mixed in.

Reviewers are **blind**: the sample does not mark injected items. The answer key (`time-study-answer-key.jsonl`,
regenerated from the seed, not committed) is held by the study lead.

**Who.** At least three people who will do the work in the staffed offer (OD-M-08). Each works a random third of the
sample, plus 20 items shared by all three to measure agreement.

**Setting.** Two settings: (a) a paper or spreadsheet walkthrough using the public sources (Pub 78, BMF, revocation list,
OFAC search) before a prototype exists; (b) the same items again on the prototype once it exists. Setting (b) is the one
that replaces the model's minutes, and (a) gives the unaided baseline. The comparison between them is the measured
AI-assistance and workflow effect.

**Record per item.** Fields: reviewer; start and end time; outcome (approve, exception decided, refuse, escalate); the
sources consulted; the facts compared (CNV-GRT-006: never "the score"); and confidence.

**Measures.**

- Median and P90 minutes per item kind.
- Accuracy against the answer key. Each injected case must end in refuse or escalate, never approve.
- Agreement between reviewers on the shared items.
- Minutes for reuse items against first-time items: this is the measured value of determination reuse.

**Acceptance.**

- Any injected case approved by a reviewer is a **finding**, and the workflow is changed before the staffed offer launches.
- Measured medians replace `minutes_per_exception`, `contact_minutes` and the illustrative reuse and operations minutes in the model, labelled "internal study" (not "measured at a sponsor").
- Claims CLM-02 and CLM-05 are restated under the 30-day rule if the rerun model moves them.

**Limits.** These are Unbound's own staff, with no sponsor's legacy systems and synthetic donors. A sponsor's staff may be
slower or faster, and real contacts are messier than generated ones. The first paying sponsor's 90-day study (report 12)
supersedes these results.

### RCD-017 — Confirm provisional decision: Funding model: currency reservation with haircut, cash sleeve, or pool buffer; and the percentage-grant fixing point.

**Disposition:** business-policy-decision · **Confidence:** medium · **Jurisdiction:** United States (federal tax law; US GAAP for not-for-profit entities) · **Batch:** B (Accounting and financial reporting)

**Original provisional answer.** Reservation against capacity computed in the same transaction. Grant states Reserved → FundingPlanned → Funded before payment. Each pool keeps a cash liquidity buffer (sponsor-set percentage, default 2% of pool value) so routine grants fund from cash without per-grant trades. Percentage grants fix their amount at approval.

**Relevant requirements.** RCF-GRT-015, RCF-FIN-022, RCC-GRT-007, RCC-FIN-008, OD-13, REQ-X-002, UNB-RCN-FIN-008, UNB-RCN-FIN-009, unbound:requirements/reconciliation/2026-11-23/data/decisions.csv, unbound:requirements/grants/MULTI-INVESTMENT-FUNDING-AND-PERCENTAGE-GRANTS.md, unbound:requirements/grants/END-TO-END-GRANT-LIFECYCLE-AND-OPERATING-MODEL.md, UNB-XIN-ECO-015 (unbound:requirements/discovery/2026-11-24-question-resolutions-accounting-banking-economics.md)

**Authoritative findings.**

- *established* — GAAP requires NFPs to disclose qualitative and quantitative information on financial assets available for general expenditure within one year, considering donor, legal, contractual and internal board limits; it prescribes no liquidity buffer level. [EV-B-015, 958-210-50-1A(a)-(b)]
- *established* — Fidelity Charitable discloses $77.3B of financial assets available within one year (of ~$85.9B net assets) and treats Level 3, NAV-measured and PIF assets as unavailable; it discloses no fixed cash-buffer percentage. [EV-B-008, Note 2 Liquidity]
- *interpretation* — No statute, regulation or accounting standard found prescribes a per-pool cash buffer percentage (e.g. 2%) for DAF sponsors; the 2% default is a business assumption. [EV-B-015, 958-210-50-1A (disclosure only)]
- *interpretation* — Grant expense/payable is recognized at a measured amount when the unconditional promise is made (720-25-25-1); a percentage grant therefore needs a fixed amount at its recognition point. [EV-B-013, 720-25-25-1 (p.39)]

**Supporting interpretation.** Atomic capacity reservation plus explicit funding states prevents over-commitment; a pool-level cash sleeve lets routine grants settle without per-grant trades. Fixing a percentage grant's amount at approval aligns the reservation, the expense measurement and the donor-visible amount.

**Competing interpretation.** A fixed 2% buffer may be too small in December peaks or for illiquid pools and too large (cash drag) for steady pools; some sponsors liquidate per grant (Fidelity sells contributed property and pools carry cash per investment objective). A percentage could instead be fixed at payment (donor expectation of 'x% of balance at payment').

**Applicability to Unbound.** Unbound configures the buffer and state machine; the sponsor's investment committee owns the buffer level as investment/liquidity policy. Unbound must not ship 2% as an unlabeled default.

**Business assumptions.**

- Grant outflows are forecastable from history by pool and season
- Pools hold daily-liquid securities except designated illiquid sleeves
- Sponsor's investment policy statement permits a cash sleeve

**Recommended answer.** Keep atomic reservation in the same transaction and Reserved -> FundingPlanned -> Funded. Replace the fixed 2% default with a sponsor-set buffer per pool calibrated from a stress model (e.g. high-percentile net outflow over the settlement horizon plus December peak), with 2% allowed only as an explicitly labeled placeholder until 12 months of data exist. Fix percentage grants at approval (the amount that is reserved, communicated and expensed), with an option to re-fix only by a new approval.

**Remaining uncertainty.** Actual outflow distributions per sponsor/pool; whether any sponsor IPS forbids cash sleeves; donor expectation for percentage grants.

**Consequences if wrong.** Buffer too small: grant payment delays and forced intraday sales; too large: cash drag on donor accounts and investment-policy deviation.

**Conservative fallback.** Per-grant liquidation with T+settlement funding when the buffer is breached; block the grant at FundingPlanned until cash settles.

**Alternative business or architecture design.** (a) Fixed % buffer: low cost, simple, but miscalibrated (exposure: payment delays/cash drag). (b) Stress-calibrated buffer (recommended): modest analytics cost, low friction, low exposure. (c) Per-grant liquidation: no cash drag but trade cost, settlement delay and ops friction; loses same-day grant payment.

**External authorization required.** Sponsor investment committee (or board) approval of the liquidity/buffer policy within the investment policy statement.

**Acceptance criteria.**

- Given two concurrent grant approvals that together exceed fund capacity, when both commit, then exactly one succeeds and the other is rejected with no over-reservation.
- Given a pool with buffer policy version v, when buffer cash falls below the configured floor, then a replenishment trade instruction is generated and an alert raised, and the policy version is recorded on the event.
- Given a percentage grant of 10% approved when fund value is $50,000, when market value changes before payment, then the payable remains $5,000.
- Given no stress calibration exists for a pool, when the buffer default is displayed, then it is labeled 'placeholder - not calibrated'.

**Reconsideration triggers.**

- Two or more buffer breaches in a quarter
- Investment committee changes IPS
- Sponsor requests fix-at-payment percentage grants

**Related questions.**

- none

**Corrections to prior records.**

- RCD-017 decided_option: States a default 2% buffer without evidence; no authority or audited sponsor disclosure supports a universal 2%. → Make buffer sponsor-set and stress-calibrated; label any 2% value as an uncalibrated placeholder.

### RCD-018 — Confirm provisional decision: Fee base and posting: on what value, when, net of what, and payable to whom?

**Disposition:** business-policy-decision · **Confidence:** medium · **Jurisdiction:** United States (federal tax law; US GAAP for not-for-profit entities) · **Batch:** B (Accounting and financial reporting)

**Original provisional answer.** Fee base is end-of-day fund market value, including reserved but unpaid amounts and excluding fees embedded in pool NAV. Fees accrue daily (actual/365) and settle monthly, with no fee on accrued fees. Minimum fees apply per sponsor schedule. Fees are recognized as sponsor revenue when settled.

**Relevant requirements.** RCF-FIN-012, RCF-FIN-013, UNB-RCN-FIN-005, UNB-ACC-010, UNB-XIN-ERP-002, unbound:requirements/FULL-ACCOUNTING-SYSTEM.md, unbound:requirements/accounting/CANONICAL-CHART-AND-POSTING-RULES.md §4 matrix row 'Sponsor fee assessed to a DAF', unbound:requirements/reconciliation/2026-11-23/data/decisions.csv

**Authoritative findings.**

- *established* — Fidelity Charitable charges 0.60% of average Giving Account assets (or $100 minimum); for pools the fee is charged to the pool pro rata daily and affects NAV; for assets not in pools (including contributed property not yet sold) the fee is charged directly to the account. [EV-B-007, Fees and Expenses - General operating expenses]
- *established* — Vanguard Charitable's 0.60% fee accrues daily, reduces pool NAV and is presented as 'Net Assets Released for Administrative Fee' used to pay operating expenses - i.e. an internal release within the same entity, not third-party revenue. [EV-B-009, Note 7]
- *established* — The Boston Foundation funds operations by administrative fees 'allocated from other fund categories based upon those funds' market value' - an internal allocation. [EV-B-011, Note 1 Operating funds; (l) Administrative Fees]
- *established* — Foundation for the Carolinas reports 'Administrative fees received' of $7.0M as revenue on $4.4B of net assets and recognizes fees in the fiscal year in which they occur (accrual), not when settled. [EV-B-012, Statement of Activities; Note 2 Administrative Fees Received]
- *interpretation* — Internal fees charged by a sponsor to its own DAFs within the same legal entity are not revenue from an external party and are eliminated/presented as internal transfers on the entity's GAAP statements; fees from agency funds (liabilities) or other entities can be revenue. [EV-B-009, Note 7 (with EV-B-012 magnitude)]

**Supporting interpretation.** Daily accrual on end-of-day market value, monthly settlement and no fee-on-fee is consistent with Fidelity/Vanguard practice (daily NAV-based accrual). Excluding fees embedded in pool NAV avoids double charging.

**Competing interpretation.** Fee base could be average assets (Fidelity), quarterly balance (Boston DAFs), or 8-quarter average (Boston endowments). Including reserved-but-unpaid grants in the base is a pricing choice; including recognized grants payable is not, because those amounts are already expensed and no longer fund net assets.

**Applicability to Unbound.** Unbound must support multiple versioned fee bases and day-count conventions and must post internal fees as interfund transfers with consolidated elimination, recognizing revenue only for fees from external payers (agency funds, partner invoices) on an accrual basis.

**Business assumptions.**

- Sponsor contracts define fee schedules
- Some sponsors host agency/pass-through funds whose fees are external revenue

**Recommended answer.** Version the fee basis per schedule (end-of-day market value default; average-balance and periodic-balance options), day count, accrual and settlement cadence, minimum, and payer source. Exclude grants already recognized as payable from the base; reserved-but-uncommunicated amounts are included by default (still fund net assets) and configurable. No fee on accrued fees; exclude fees embedded in pool NAV. Accounting: fees charged to the sponsor's own DAFs are internal transfers (fund-level expense / operating-fund recovery) eliminated in entity statements; fees from agency funds or other external payers are revenue recognized as the service period elapses (accrual), not on settlement.

**Remaining uncertainty.** Each sponsor auditor's presentation choice (gross internal fee lines vs net release); ASC 606 analysis for external fee revenue was not researched in depth.

**Consequences if wrong.** Recognizing internal fees as revenue overstates revenue and expense; cash-basis recognition misstates period results.

**Conservative fallback.** Post internal fees only as fund-dimension transfers with a zero entity-level P&L effect; treat external fee revenue on accrual with CPA review.

**Alternative business or architecture design.** (a) Single hard-coded base: cheapest, but fails sponsor contracts. (b) Versioned FeeBase contract (recommended): moderate build, low exposure. (c) Delegate fee calc to custodian NAV only: no platform calc, but loses per-account minimums/tiers and fund-level transparency.

**External authorization required.** Executed sponsor fee schedule / program terms; sponsor controller-approved fee accounting policy.

**Acceptance criteria.**

- Given a DAF with fees charged by its own sponsor, when entity-level statements are generated, then fee entries net to zero across funds (eliminated) and no revenue line is produced.
- Given an agency fund charged a fee for March, when March closes unpaid, then fee revenue and a receivable are recognized in March.
- Given a grant recognized as payable, when the daily fee base is computed, then the payable amount is excluded.
- Given a fee schedule change effective on date D, when fees are computed across D, then each day uses the version in force that day.

**Reconsideration triggers.**

- Sponsor auditor requires different presentation
- New fee type with external payer

**Related questions.**

- none

**Corrections to prior records.**

- RCD-018 decided_option: 'Fees are recognized as sponsor revenue when settled' conflicts with GAAP accrual and with audited practice: internal DAF fees are internal releases/transfers (Vanguard Note 7; Boston allocations), and FFTC recognizes fees in the year they occur. → Internal fees: interfund transfer, eliminated; external fees: revenue on accrual over the service period.
- RCD-018 fee base: Including amounts already recognized as grants payable would charge fees on assets no longer in fund net assets. → Distinguish reserved (included, configurable) from recognized payable (excluded).

### RCD-019 — Confirm provisional decision: Contribution valuation, completed-gift dates, acknowledgment timing and returned contributions.

**Disposition:** reasoned-interpretation · **Confidence:** medium · **Jurisdiction:** United States (federal tax law; US GAAP for not-for-profit entities) · **Batch:** B (Accounting and financial reporting)

**Original provisional answer.** Contributions credit grantable capacity only on Final: ACH after the sponsor-set return window (default 5 business days); wire on receipt; check on clear; securities on settlement into custody. Acknowledgment is issued on CompletedGiftDetermined ∧ SponsorAccepted. Accepted assets never return to a donor; returned grant funds go back to the originating fund.

**Relevant requirements.** RCF-FIN-001, RCF-FIN-002, RCF-FIN-004, RCF-FIN-007, RCF-FIN-020, RCF-FIN-021, RCF-REG-004, RR-UNB-ACC-006-r1, UNB-RCN-FIN-001, UNB-RCN-FIN-012, UNB-RCN-FIN-013, unbound:requirements/FULL-ACCOUNTING-SYSTEM.md, unbound:requirements/accounting/CANONICAL-CHART-AND-POSTING-RULES.md, unbound:requirements/reconciliation/2026-11-23/data/decisions.csv

**Authoritative findings.**

- *established* — A contribution is ordinarily made when delivery is effected; a mailed check that clears in due course is made on the mailing date. [EV-B-001, §1.170A-1(b)]
- *established* — An endorsed stock certificate is completed on delivery/mailing to the donee; if delivered to the donor's bank/broker or issuer for transfer, the gift is complete when transferred on the corporation's books. [EV-B-001, §1.170A-1(b)]
- *established* — Credit card gifts are deductible in the year charged; pay-by-phone gifts are delivered on the date the financial institution pays. [EV-B-002, 'When To Deduct']
- *established* — DAF contributions are deductible only with a CWA from the sponsor stating it has exclusive legal control; CWAs for >=$250 must state cash amount and describe (not value) noncash property, and be obtained by the earlier of the filing date or due date. - *established* — Donor tax FMV of traded securities is the mean of high and low selling prices on the valuation date - distinct from GAAP fair value used by the sponsor. [EV-B-004, 'Stocks and Bonds']
- *established* — Fidelity treats EFT contributions as timely if initiated by Dec 31 and wires/securities if received by Dec 31 (guidelines). [EV-B-006, deadline table]
- *established* — Fidelity records contributions on the date received and accepted; property not accepted is returned; accepted contributions are irrevocable and non-refundable. [EV-B-008, Note 2 Donor Contributions; EV-B-007 Contributions - General]

**Supporting interpretation.** Gift completion (tax date), GAAP recognition (received/accepted), availability for granting (risk of reversal) and acknowledgment are four separate facts. For donor-initiated ACH (credit from donor bank or sponsor-pulled debit) no regulation fixes the date; by analogy to pay-by-phone, the gift is made when the donor's bank pays (settlement), with initiation date shown as a sponsor guideline (Fidelity). Card: date charged.

**Competing interpretation.** Some practitioners treat ACH debit gifts as made on the authorization/initiation date (Fidelity's year-end guideline) - a donor-favorable reading not confirmed by regulation. Electronic DTC securities transfers: date received in the sponsor's account (books-transfer analog) vs date donor instructed.

**Business assumptions.**

- Sponsor accepts ACH, wire, check, card, DTC securities
- Sponsor reserves acceptance discretion (as Fidelity)

**Recommended answer.** Store separately: GiftCompletionDate (tax: check=mailing/postmark date if cleared; certificate=delivery/mailing; broker/DTC=date credited to sponsor account; card=charge date; ACH=settlement date with initiation date retained; wire=receipt), AcceptanceDate (GAAP recognition), AvailabilityDate (sponsor risk rule: e.g. ACH default 5 business days configurable, card per dispute risk; explicitly not finality), and AcknowledgmentDate (issued after acceptance; noncash described without value). If an ACH/card payment is later returned or charged back, reverse the contribution (it was never completed) and void/amend the acknowledgment; this is not a 'refund' of an accepted asset. Accepted assets are never refunded to donors; returned grant funds go back to the originating fund.

**Remaining uncertainty.** No IRS guidance explicitly fixes the gift date for ACH debits or DTC transfers; card-network dispute windows not verified here.

**Consequences if wrong.** Wrong tax year on acknowledgments (donor deduction disallowed or amended), grants funded from reversed money.

**Conservative fallback.** Acknowledge using settlement/receipt date; keep capacity unavailable until the sponsor's risk window passes; flag Dec 31/Jan 1 boundary items for manual review.

**Alternative business or architecture design.** (a) Single 'Final' date (original): simple but conflates tax date with availability (high exposure on year-end). (b) Four-date model (recommended): small schema cost, low exposure. (c) Accept only wires/DTC for large gifts: removes reversal risk but adds donor friction and loses card/ACH gifts.

**External authorization required.** Sponsor counsel/CPA sign-off of the completed-gift rule table and acknowledgment template (sponsor is the legal issuer).

**Acceptance criteria.**

- Given a check postmarked Dec 31 and deposited Jan 3 that clears, when the acknowledgment is generated, then the gift date is Dec 31.
- Given DTC shares credited to the sponsor account on Jan 2 after a Dec 30 instruction, when acknowledged, then the gift date is Jan 2 and the instruction date is retained.
- Given an ACH gift returned R10 on day 40, when the return posts, then the contribution and capacity are reversed and the acknowledgment is voided or amended.
- Given a noncash gift >= $250, when the CWA is generated, then it describes the property without stating a tax value and states exclusive legal control.

### XID-ADV-006 — Confirm provisional decision: Which GrantRecognitionPoint values are permitted?

**Disposition:** reasoned-interpretation · **Confidence:** medium · **Jurisdiction:** United States (federal tax law; US GAAP for not-for-profit entities) · **Batch:** B (Accounting and financial reporting)

**Original provisional answer.** Permitted GrantRecognitionPoint values: ApprovalCommunicatedToGrantee (default) and PaymentReleased (only where payment is the first communication to the grantee). SponsorApproval is NOT a selectable policy; it collapses into ApprovalCommunicatedToGrantee when the approval notice is sent in the same period. **Relevant requirements.** UNB-XIN-ACC-002, UNB-ACC-003, UNB-ACC-033, ACC-MDL-002, unbound:requirements/FULL-ACCOUNTING-SYSTEM.md, unbound:requirements/accounting/CANONICAL-CHART-AND-POSTING-RULES.md, unbound:requirements/discovery/2026-11-23-cross-industry/data/decisions.csv

**Authoritative findings.**

- *established* — Contributions made are expensed in the period made; unconditional promises to give cash are recognized as payables and contribution expense. [EV-B-013, 720-25-25-1 (ASU 2018-08 pp.39-40)]
- *established* — A donor-imposed condition requires a barrier and a right of return/release; ambiguous stipulations are presumed conditional; conditional promises are not recognized until the barrier is overcome. [EV-B-013, 958-605-25-5A to 25-5E]
- *established* — Pre-2018 Codification text (958-720-25-2, superseded by ASU 2018-08) said an unconditional promise is recognized when the donor has an obligation, which 'generally occurs when the donor approves a specific grant or when the recipient of the promise is notified'; donor-reserved rescission rights prevented recognition. [EV-B-013, 958-720-25-2 superseded text (p.36)]
- *established* — Audited sponsor practice recognizes unconditional grants when approved: Colorado Gives ('when they are approved'), Boston Foundation ('when approved by the Board or committed to grantees'), FFTC ('when approved and all conditions have been met'), Vanguard (expense when authorized). [EV-B-010, Note 2 Grants (also EV-B-011 (i), EV-B-012 Note 9, EV-B-009 Note 2)]
- *interpretation* — A promise to give is an agreement; recognition requires verifiable documentation that the promise was made and received, implying communication to the recipient. [EV-B-028, article body]

**Supporting interpretation.** Requiring communication prevents expensing internal approvals that the sponsor can still freely cancel; Unbound sends the notice at approval, so approval and communication fall in the same period in normal operation.

**Competing interpretation.** The removed Codification text and current audited practice accept approval as the recognition event where the sponsor's approval creates an obligation; DAF sponsors approve grants for payment within days and rarely rescind. Removing SponsorApproval as a selectable policy contradicts majority audited practice.

**Applicability to Unbound.** Unbound must not hard-code an accounting policy that differs from sponsors' audited policies; it must capture approval, communication, conditions and rescission rights independently and let the sponsor's policy choose the trigger.

**Business assumptions.**

- Unbound auto-sends approval notices
- Sponsors retain the right to cancel before payment in program terms

**Recommended answer.** Permitted values: SponsorApproval (allowed where the sponsor's CPA-documented policy treats approval as creating the obligation and the sponsor does not reserve a free rescission right), ApprovalCommunicatedToGrantee (platform default), PaymentReleased (only where payment is the first communication). Conditional grants recognized when the barrier is overcome. Always capture approval, communication, condition and rescission evidence; produce a period-end cutoff report of approved-but-uncommunicated items.

**Remaining uncertainty.** Current Codification wording of 958-605/958-405/958-720 on recognition timing not read (paywalled); auditor positions vary.

**Consequences if wrong.** Expense/payable cutoff misstatements at year end (usually small because approval-to-payment is days).

**Conservative fallback.** Default ApprovalCommunicatedToGrantee with same-moment notification, eliminating the timing difference.

**Alternative business or architecture design.** (a) Single fixed trigger: simple but conflicts with some audited policies. (b) Policy enumeration with evidence capture (recommended). (c) Make approval and notification atomic so the choice is immaterial: low cost, removes most exposure; loses only deferred-notice workflows.

**External authorization required.** Sponsor management's written grant-recognition accounting policy, accepted by the sponsor's external auditor in the first audited year.

**Acceptance criteria.**

- Given policy ApprovalCommunicatedToGrantee and a grant approved Dec 31 with notice sent Jan 2, when Dec closes, then no expense is recognized in Dec and the item appears on the cutoff report.
- Given policy SponsorApproval without CPA policy evidence attached, when a sponsor tries to activate it, then activation is blocked.
- Given a grant with a measurable barrier and right of release, when approved, then no expense is recognized until the barrier is recorded as met.

**Reconsideration triggers.**

- Sponsor auditor policy statement
- FASB amendment to 958-605/720-25

**Related questions.**

- none

### XID-TRS-001 — Confirm provisional decision: Can an agent under POA, guardian or conservator exercise DAF advisory privileges?

**Disposition:** reasoned-interpretation · **Confidence:** medium · **Jurisdiction:** State law (UPOAA in roughly 26-30 states; non-uniform POA statutes elsewhere, e.g. FL, NY, CA); state guardianship/conservatorship law (UGCOPAA enactments, older UGPPA); federal §4966 for DAF definition · **Batch:** E (Governance, succession and delegated authority)

**Original provisional answer.** An agent under a power of attorney may exercise advisory privileges only if the instrument expressly grants authority to make gifts or charitable donations, the sponsor's program terms allow representatives, and the instrument is verified. Guardians and conservators act only under a court order that covers charitable gifts. Otherwise the privileges are suspended pending succession or court order.

**Relevant requirements.** UNB-XIN-TRS-004, REQ-X-068, /home/user/unbound/requirements/discovery/2026-11-23-cross-industry/data/decisions.csv, /home/user/unbound/requirements/discovery/2026-11-24-final-55-question-resolutions.md

**Authoritative findings.**

- *established* — UPOAA §201(a): an agent may make a gift on behalf of the principal or with the principal's property only if the POA expressly grants that authority AND exercise is not otherwise prohibited by another agreement or instrument to which the authority or property is subject. [EV-E-009, UPOAA §201(a)(2) and lead-in]
- *established* — UPOAA §120: a person presented with an acknowledged POA must accept it or request certification/translation/opinion within seven business days unless a refusal ground applies (e.g., not otherwise required to transact with the principal in the same circumstances; good-faith belief agent lacks authority; APS report); wrongful refusal risks a court order and fees. [EV-E-009, UPOAA §120(a)-(c)]
- *established* — NC enactment varies the uniform text: general gift authority reaches charitable gifts only 'in accordance with the principal's history of making or joining in the making of gifts'. [EV-E-010, G.S. 32C-2-217(b)(1)b]
- *established* — Conservators need specific court authorization, after notice, to make non-de-minimis gifts (UGCOPAA enactment, Maine). [EV-E-012, 18-C MRSA §5-414(1)(A)]
- *interpretation* — DAF assets are owned and controlled by the sponsor; advisory privileges arise from donor status or donor appointment, so a DAF grant recommendation is not a gift of the principal's property. [EV-E-005, §4966(d)(2)(A)]
- *established* — Vanguard accepts a copy of a durable POA as written proof of the advisor's incapacity, which triggers the succession plan rather than conferring privileges on the agent. [EV-E-002, Account structure 'Successor advisor'; Enacting a succession plan]
- *established* — DAFgiving360: an executor or trustee cannot exercise account privileges unless also a named successor; Fidelity: only Account Holders and Authorized Persons designated by an Account Holder exercise privileges, and Fidelity may decline or revoke any designation. [EV-E-003, p.31 n.14; EV-E-001 'Fidelity Charitable approval', 'Authorized Person']
- *established* — Proposed regs would treat any person appointed or designated by a donor, with no particular form, as a donor-advisor (relevant to §4967/§4958 status of agents) - proposed only, not effective as of 2026-11-24. [EV-E-007, Prop. §53.4966-1(h)(1); EV-E-008]

**Supporting interpretation.** Because the sponsor owns the assets and the privilege is contractual, the governing question is what the program terms allow. UPOAA §201(a) itself defers to 'another agreement ... to which the authority ... is subject', so sponsor terms that limit representative authority are effective in UPOAA states. Requiring express gift/charitable authority is a sound proxy for the principal's intent, but the strictly legal hook is the program terms. A sponsor that never accepts agents (only donor-designated Authorized Persons) is consistent with Fidelity, DAFgiving360 and Vanguard practice.

**Competing interpretation.** An agent with broad general authority could argue that recommending grants from a DAF is not a 'gift' (the gift was made at contribution) and so does not need express gift authority; and UPOAA §120 could be read to require the sponsor to accept an acknowledged POA within 7 business days. Counter: §120(b)(1) excuses acceptance where the person is not otherwise required to transact with the principal in the same circumstances; the sponsor never must act on a recommendation. The sponsor's terms should say expressly that agents act only as the program allows. States outside UPOAA (e.g., Florida §709.2202 requires principal's signature/initials next to gift powers - recalled, not fetched) can be stricter.

**Applicability to Unbound.** Unbound should implement a representative-authority record (instrument type, jurisdiction, acknowledgment, scope flags incl. express gift/charitable authority, court order scope, expiry) and a 7-business-day POA response clock with statutory refusal-reason codes, while the sponsor's terms decide whether agents may act at all.

**Business assumptions.**

- Sponsor program terms will state whether agents, guardians and conservators may exercise privileges.
- Sponsors will want to avoid UPOAA §120 refusal liability by responding within 7 business days with a documented ground.

**Recommended answer.** Confirm with refinements: (1) Default: an agent under a POA may exercise advisory privileges only if (a) the sponsor's program terms permit representatives, (b) the instrument is acknowledged and verified, and (c) it expressly grants authority to make gifts or charitable donations (or expressly references the DAF/advisory privileges); exercise must follow the principal's giving history and may not benefit the agent. Contributions by an agent always require express gift authority (UPOAA §§201, 217). (2) Guardians of the person have no authority; conservators act only under a court order specifically authorizing charitable gifts or DAF advisory acts. (3) Otherwise treat the POA/court order as evidence of incapacity, suspend the principal's privileges, and activate the next named advisor/successor per program terms (Vanguard pattern). (4) Respond to any presented POA within 7 business days (accept, request certification, or refuse with a §120 ground) and log it. (5) The agent's acts are logged as the agent's, and the agent is treated as a donor-advisor for impermissible-benefit screening.

**Remaining uncertainty.** No case law or IRS guidance found on agents exercising DAF advisory privileges; state variations (non-UPOAA states, NC-style charitable-history limits, Florida execution formalities) not fully surveyed.

**Consequences if wrong.** Accepting an agent without authority could facilitate elder exploitation and expose the sponsor to claims; refusing without a statutory ground in a UPOAA state could bring a court order and fee award.

**Conservative fallback.** Program terms state that representatives may not exercise advisory privileges; POAs and court orders are used only to verify incapacity and trigger succession; respond within 7 business days citing §120(b)(1)-type ground.

**Alternative business or architecture design.** (A) Donor-designated Authorized Person/interested-party roles set while the donor has capacity (Fidelity/Vanguard pattern) instead of accepting POAs: lowest exposure, modest friction (donor must act in advance), small lost opportunity for incapacitated donors without designees. (B) Accept agents with express authority (recommended default): medium exposure, needs instrument review (~30 min per case). (C) Accept any general POA: highest exposure; rejected.

**External authorization required.** Sponsor program-terms clause on representatives approved by the sponsor; for conservators, a court order specifically authorizing the act.

**Acceptance criteria.**

- Given an acknowledged POA is uploaded, When 7 business days elapse without a decision, Then the case escalates and the clock state is visible to the sponsor.
- Given a POA without express gift or charitable authority, When the agent recommends a grant, Then the recommendation is blocked with reason code REPRESENTATIVE_AUTHORITY_INSUFFICIENT.

- Given an agent-recommended grant would benefit the agent or their dependants, Then it is blocked and routed to compliance.
- Given sponsor terms disallow representatives, When any POA is presented, Then the platform records a refusal citing the program-terms ground within 7 business days.

**Disposition:** reasoned-interpretation · **Confidence:** high · **Jurisdiction:** US-federal (TCPA/FCC); federal circuits split on texts under 227(c) · **Batch:** F (Privacy, communications and sanctions)

**Relevant requirements.** UNB-XIN-REG-003, UNB-XIN-CRM-003

**Authoritative findings.**

- *established* — The regulation extends the nonprofit standard to calls 'made by or on behalf of a tax-exempt nonprofit organization': advertising/telemarketing robocalls to wireless numbers need only prior express consent (not written). [EV-F-019, 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(2)]
- *established* — Residential prerecorded calls by or on behalf of a nonprofit are exempt if no more than three per 30 days and opt-outs are honored; nonprofit calls are excluded from 'telephone solicitation'. [EV-F-019, 64.1200(a)(3)(iv); (f)(15)(iii)]
- *established* — The statute's 'telephone solicitation' exclusion covers calls 'by a tax exempt nonprofit organization'; the 'on behalf of' extension is in the FCC rule, not the statutory text. [EV-F-020, 47 U.S.C. 227(a)(4)(C)]
- *established* — ATDS or prerecorded/artificial-voice calls to wireless numbers require prior express consent with no nonprofit exception. [EV-F-020, 227(b)(1)(A)(iii)]
- *interpretation* — Text messages qualify as calls under 227(b)(1)(A)(iii) (stated as undisputed by the Supreme Court). [EV-F-025, 577 U.S. at 156 (not fetched)]
- *established* — Seventh Circuit (July 2026) held marketing texts are not 'telephone calls' under 227(c)(5) (DNC private claims), applying McLaughlin (2025); 227(b) text claims unaffected. [EV-F-026, H&K summary of Steidinger]
- *established* — One-to-one consent and 'logically and topically related' requirements vacated (11th Cir. Jan 24, 2025); FCC removed the text effective with the April 30, 2025 mandate. [EV-F-023, 90 FR 42137 abstract]
- *established* — From Nov 25, 2026, revocation of informational robocalls/texts may be treated as category-specific; revocation of advertising/telemarketing covers all such messages from that caller; callers may designate exclusive revocation means; honor within 10 business days. [EV-F-021, 91 FR 67214; amended 64.1200(a)(10)-(12)]
- *established* — FCC is considering shorter revocation timing, mandatory revoke-all and affiliate treatment (proposed only). [EV-F-022, FNPRM abstract]

**Supporting interpretation.** The regulation's 'on behalf of' language covers an agent or vendor (including a for-profit operator) sending for the tax-exempt sponsor's own program. So for the sponsor's messages, prior express consent suffices even where content is promotional. A for-profit partner's own marketing does not qualify.

**Competing interpretation.** After McLaughlin Chiropractic v. McKesson (2025), district courts are not bound by FCC interpretations, and the statute's 'telephone solicitation' exclusion says only 'by a tax exempt nonprofit'. A court could therefore deny the exemption to a for-profit vendor's calls. Separately, the 'nonprofit' exemptions do not relieve the wireless-number consent requirement for ATDS/prerecorded messages.

**Applicability to Unbound.** Unbound sends SMS and possibly prerecorded voice for sponsors. Most texts go to wireless numbers, where prior express consent is needed regardless. The practical gap between prior express consent and prior express written consent can be closed cheaply at onboarding.

**Business assumptions.**

- Sponsor is a 501(c)(3) and messages concern its own DAF program.
- Unbound's SMS platform may or may not be an ATDS under Facebook v. Duguid (not analysed here).

**Recommended answer.** Confirm with hardening. (1) Capture consent at onboarding in a form that meets prior express written consent (signed e-consent naming the sponsor, stating it is not a condition of service) for every automated/prerecorded text or call. This makes the 'on behalf of' question irrelevant. (2) Messages advertising a for-profit partner require written consent naming that partner. (3) Residential prerecorded calls: limit to 3 per 30 days, keep a company-specific DNC list and give identification. (4) Implement revocation per the rules effective Nov 25, 2026: either designate an exclusive revocation means and disclose it in each text, or accept any reasonable means. Treat informational revocations per category and advertising revocations as all-advertising. Honor within 10 business days (target: immediate). (5) Track the FNPRM.

**Remaining uncertainty.** Judicial acceptance of 'on behalf of' for for-profit vendors after McLaughlin; ATDS status of Unbound's sending stack; Seventh Circuit vs other circuits on texts under 227(c).

**Consequences if wrong.** TCPA statutory damages of $500-$1,500 per message, with class-action exposure.

**Conservative fallback.** Written-standard consent for all automated messages; no prerecorded voice in R1.

**Alternative business or architecture design.** Avoidance: R1 SMS only for transactional/informational messages the donor triggered (one-time codes, grant status) with written-standard consent captured in the onboarding flow; no marketing SMS until counsel review. Cost: low. Friction: one checkbox. Exposure: minimal. Lost opportunity: SMS marketing channel deferred.

**External authorization required.** none

- Given a text reply 'STOP' to an advertising message, when processed, then all advertising messages from that caller are suppressed within 10 business days (target within 1 minute) and one confirmation text without marketing is sent within 5 minutes.

**Related questions.**

**Authoritative findings.**

- *established* — Federal law permits recording by a party or with one party's consent (absent criminal/tortious purpose). [EV-F-035, 18 U.S.C. 2511(2)(d)]
- *established* — California applies Penal Code 632 to out-of-state businesses recording calls with California residents; advising all parties at the outset of the intent to record defeats a 632 violation, because a party may decline to continue. [EV-F-033, Kearney, 39 Cal.4th 95 (opinion text)]
- *interpretation* — Penal Code 632.7 bars parties and non-parties from recording covered cellular/cordless calls without all parties' consent. [EV-F-034, Smith v. LoanMe (summaries; not fetched)]
- *established* — All-party consent states per RCFP: CA, DE, FL, IL, MD, MA, MI (qualified), MT, NH, PA, WA; CT and NV have phone-specific nuances; for interstate calls assume the stricter law applies. [EV-F-036, RCFP Introduction]

**Supporting interpretation.** No recording at R1 avoids the issue entirely. If recording is enabled later, a uniform disclosure at the start of every call plus explicit affirmative consent satisfies the strictest states. Kearney shows that an outset disclosure alone suffices in California.

**Competing interpretation.** Continuation-after-notice is widely treated as implied consent, but not uniformly across all-party states. AI transcription vendors have faced CIPA s.631 'third-party eavesdropper' claims (not verified here).

**Applicability to Unbound.** Unbound telephony adapters serve donors in all states.

**Business assumptions.**

- Recording is not needed for R1 operations.

**Recommended answer.** Confirm: no call recording or machine transcription at R1. When enabling: (1) disclose at the outset to every party, including parties added later; (2) require affirmative consent (DTMF or verbal yes); (3) provide a no-recording path; (4) contract the transcription vendor as a service provider with no training use and no independent use, to reduce CIPA s.631 third-party exposure. Maintain a jurisdiction table (state, statute, all/one-party, medium nuance, last verified date) for review, but apply the uniform strictest rule.

**Remaining uncertainty.** State list secondary (RCFP); vendor-eavesdropper case law not reviewed.

**Consequences if wrong.** Recording without all-party consent risks CIPA statutory damages ($5,000 per violation under 637.2, from memory) and class actions.

**Conservative fallback.** Recording disabled.

**Alternative business or architecture design.** Avoidance (current decision): recording disabled. Cost: loses QA/training data and dispute evidence. Exposure: none. Alternative: chat/email channels with written transcripts by design (consent through terms) for QA.

**External authorization required.** none

**Acceptance criteria.**

- Given R1 configuration, when any call connects, then no audio is recorded or streamed to a transcription service.
- Given recording enabled later, when a party does not affirmatively consent, then recording does not start and the call continues unrecorded.

**Reconsideration triggers.**

- Sponsor requests recording
- State law changes

**Related questions.**

- **UNB-XIN-SVC-003#Q1**: Not established for all target states. Kearney (Cal. 2006) holds that an outset advisement suffices in California, since a party may decline to continue. Other all-party states are not uniformly settled, so require explicit affirmative consent.
- **UNB-XIN-SVC-003#Q2**: Yes for any newly joining party. All-party statutes require the consent of all parties, so a third party added by transfer or conference must hear the disclosure and consent before recording continues with them.

# Enterprise-wide decision inventory: deterministic first, AI only when needed, humans for exceptions

Date: 2026-11-23. **Theoretical requirements and adversarial acceptance specifications; no tests executed or formal governance approval.** Companion: `ADVERSARIAL-VALIDATION-AND-TIERED-DECISION-ARCHITECTURE-2026-11-23.md`, REQ-X-017.

## Global routing contract

Every decision has a verified actor, sponsor/tenant, policy version, authoritative evidence snapshot, applicable rule trace, decision ID, risk/authority class, current holds and output status. **Tier 1** evaluates complete typed facts with deterministic rules, including explicit Unknown and hard-block outcomes. Conclusive authorized cases end at Tier 1 with no AI call and no human work. **Tier 2** runs only on specific evidence ambiguity or unstructured-input triggers, under cost, latency, data and authority limits. AI normally gathers evidence and recommends; it cannot override holds or authorize consequential irreversible decisions. **Tier 3** is an independently authorized human reviewer only when permitted judgment or law requires one. Human escalation never overrides nonwaivable prohibitions. No self-approval by a person, agent or materially involved policy author.

**Statuses:** `ResolvedByRule`, `RejectedByRule`, `HeldForEvidence`, `HeldForRisk`, `AIInvestigationPending`, `HumanReviewRequired`, `ApprovedByIndependentAuthority`, `QueuedIntakeOnly`, `UnknownExternalOutcome`. No generic `Accepted` implies sponsor approval, financial commitment or settlement.

**Decision routing:** Rule success/rejection/hold ends or safely pauses the flow. If facts missing, request evidence without AI where possible. Invoke AI only if ambiguity remains and the authorized action benefits from interpretation. If AI cannot resolve evidence within bounds, route to an independent reviewer if judgment is lawful, otherwise hold. Never let an AI or human retry loop transform a hard prohibition into approval. Sponsor-specific grant rules are distinct from one shared charity-vetting standard.

## Authority matrix and escalation triggers

- **Deterministic authority:** Sponsor board/counsel-validated policy delegation only, separately approved publication, pinned versions, signed rule trace, independent maker-checker for material policy changes. Shared charity vetting is centrally governed and reusable; sponsor grant authorization remains distinct.
- **AI authority:** Read, classify, compare, recommend, draft evidence requests and bounded reversible nonfinancial administrative decisions only if separately authorized. No autonomous grant/payment/sanctions release, fee waiver, policy publication, hold removal or exception approval. Model output never substitutes for sponsor authorization.
- **Human authority:** Independently approved sponsor role and delegation; legal, compliance, finance, custody and incident exceptions according to authoritative DoA. Reviewer may not have originated or materially changed the item. No general override for hard stops.
- **Trigger to AI:** Ambiguous entity match; unstructured purpose/document; conflicting third-party evidence; unusual pattern not resolved by rules; risk anomaly requiring interpretation. Skip AI when deterministic rejection, hold or request-for-information is sufficient.
- **Trigger to human:** Legal judgment; unresolved contradictory evidence; suspected fraud; policy exception; material irreversible risk; required signoff; AI failure/uncertainty only if a human is allowed to decide. Otherwise hold and request more evidence.

## Cross-cutting acceptance and adversarial validation

1. **No unnecessary AI:** For every complete, safe, rule-resolvable test case, assert zero model invocations and zero human assignments. AI and human usage are observable by decision ID.
2. **No unsafe escalation:** Missing critical facts never default to false; hard prohibitions remain blocked at all tiers, including model hallucination and reviewer override attempts.
3. **No self-approval:** Test originator as agent, staff, delegated account and policy author; independent reviewer cannot share controlling principal or approve material changes they made.
4. **No double financial effects:** Concurrent grants, payment retries, failover, stale failback and replay maintain stable idempotency, authoritative quorum and atomic reservations.
5. **No opaque payment destinations:** Provider fingerprint alone fails; charity ownership and execution destination independently verified; changed destination requires separate independent authorization.
6. **No cross-sponsor duplication or leakage:** Common charity evidence is reused; grant restrictions remain sponsor-specific; private donor/tenant data is isolated; adverse changes propagate platform-wide.
7. **No stale execution:** Each queued or recurring instance rechecks authority, sanctions, charity verification, sponsor rules, capacity and holds at execution.
8. **No false status:** UI, API and widgets distinguish submitted, queued, approved, reserved, paid, Unknown and returned; outage queue receipt is not financial acceptance.
9. **No unbounded AI:** Enforce per-case token, cost, time, retry and tool scopes; model outage never becomes a fail-open path.
10. **No rubber stamping:** Human case packet shows original evidence, rule trace, uncertainty, counterevidence and AI recommendation separately; measure independent reversals and reviewer disagreement.
11. **No policy drift:** Historical decisions reconstruct original policy and evidence versions; changes have maker-checker approvals and cannot silently rewrite prior records.
12. **No unsafe recovery:** Independent intake durability, quorum loss, protective holds, emergency controls and provider Unknown are tested under injected failures.

## Efficiency measurement and improvement loop

Instrument every decision: volume, straight-through rules-only resolution, AI escalation share, human escalation share, ultimate human touch rate, false-approval and false-hold rates, appeals/overturns, time-to-resolution p50/p95/p99, token spend, cost per completed case, evidence requests, backlog age, provider failures, reversals and incidents. Segment by sponsor, charity class, decision type and risk, without disclosing private tenant data. Illustrative 90/8/2 distribution is NOT a target or forecast. Baseline actual volumes and error rates before setting thresholds. Repeated safe exceptions may be proposed as new deterministic rules, independently reviewed, adversarially tested and version-published. Model fine-tuning or prompt changes do not silently alter decision authority.

## Priority and unresolved gates

P0: money movement, sanctions, charity eligibility, authority, policy publication, payment destination, grant capacity, DR and emergency controls. P1: contributions, accounting, AUM, noncash assets, donor succession, manual check custody, embedded widgets and privacy. P2: communications, customer support optimization, optional AI convenience. These are design priorities, not delivery commitments. Counsel must validate sponsor policy delegation, cross-sponsor legal reliance and special charity classes; finance must approve recognition/loss policies; payment providers must demonstrate independently verifiable destination; engineering must test DR quorum, queue durability and failback. Original source requirements remain authoritative until reconciled and migrated.
